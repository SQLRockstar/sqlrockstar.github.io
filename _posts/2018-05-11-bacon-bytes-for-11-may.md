Welcome to the latest edition of Bacon Bytes. This week we talk a bit about how you can't trust anything on the internet and how to make money playing the ponies.

&nbsp;
<h2>Using statistical models to win almost $1B in horse-race gambling</h2>
The next time someone tells you that data science isn't a real thing, show them <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-05-03/the-gambler-who-cracked-the-horse-racing-code" target="_blank" rel="noopener">this article about Bill Benter</a>. He built a predictive model that allowed him to maximize his winnings at the horse track, amassing almost one billion dollars.

&nbsp;
<h2>Amazon's Fake Review Problem</h2>
I'm starting to think I can't trust anything I find on the internet. Amazon is almost worth a trillion dollars now, and with millions of product reviews, it's easy to understand that a percentage of them <a href="https://www.buzzfeed.com/nicolenguyen/amazon-fake-review-problem?utm_term=.cpqbDLDpEx#.xhmG3O304p" target="_blank" rel="noopener">may not be legit</a>.

&nbsp;
<h2>Tidal accused of deliberately faking Kanye West and Beyonce streaming numbers</h2>
As I was just saying, it's getting hard to trust anything I find on the internet. In the "Likes" economy, we are going to start discovering more occurrences of where <a href="https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/did-tidal-falsify-streams-to-bulk-up-kanye-west-and-beyonce-numbers/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">people are manipulating the system</a> to be seen as more successful or popular than they really are. To some people, this is just called marketing. To the rest of us, it's dishonest.

&nbsp;
<h2>GM plans to spend $1 billion to develop self-driving cars</h2>
With projections of a $285 billion dollar market by 2030, it makes sense that companies such as GM are investing heavily in autonomous vehicles. <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-05-07/who-s-winning-the-self-driving-car-race" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Waymo is still the leader, though</a>. Which means humanity may have driverless cars because two kids from Stanford wanted a better way to search the internet.

&nbsp;
<h2>Airlines earn $4.6 billion from baggage fees last year</h2>
Which means we can expect baggage fees to increase because <a href="http://www.latimes.com/business/la-fi-airline-profits-20180507-story.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">airlines enjoy making money</a>.

&nbsp;
<h2>Microsoft’s new IntelliCode is a smarter IntelliSense</h2>
One of the announcements at Build this week was IntelliCode, which is the <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2018/05/07/microsofts-new-intellicode-is-a-smarter-intellisense/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">next generation of IntelliSense</a>. Using machine learning, developers can now get more insight into the code they are writing. Here's hoping InetlliCode makes its way into T-SQL tools at some point.

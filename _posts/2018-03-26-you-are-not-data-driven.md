Many people (and companies) say they are "data-driven". Unfortunately, what that means is "hey, I think this is true, go get the data that supports my idea. No, not that data, get the other data." This is the common definition I see on a regular basis. If this describes you (or your company), then you are not data-driven.

What it means to be data-driven is this: You collect and use data to make your decisions. I've seen this also <a href="https://medium.freecodecamp.org/which-is-better-data-driven-or-data-informed-cba4d2c9cf42" target="_blank" rel="noopener">called "data-informed"</a>, but the point here is to understand the differences in the behavior. You are not data-driven if:

- Team members withhold data from others
- Team members are not able to get insights from data
- Team members do not know what core metrics are important for success

Again, if those items describe you (or your company), then you are not data-driven. You are fooling yourself, and others.

&nbsp;
<h2>How To Use Data in a Data-Driven Company</h2>
I see someone as data-driven if they are using data as part of a process that is similar to the scientific method. Here's how the statistician <a href="http://www.jstor.org/stable/2286841?seq=1#page_scan_tab_contents" target="_blank" rel="noopener">George Box described the process in a 1976 article called Science and Statistics</a>:

<a href="https://thomaslarock.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/iterative.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-18839" src="https://thomaslarock.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/iterative-600x244.jpg" alt="George Box Science and Statistics" width="600" height="244" /></a>

I've borrowed the image from this post regarding how <a href="https://medium.com/netflix-techblog/a-b-testing-and-beyond-improving-the-netflix-streaming-experience-with-experimentation-and-data-5b0ae9295bdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Netflix does A/B testing for their streaming service to improve customer experience</a>. In that post, they discuss how Professor Box outlined in his article the use of deduction, data, induction, and theory in order to arrive at a conclusion that can best be described as "data-driven judgment".

This is what a data-driven company does, every day. They have ideas, they collect data, they refine their ideas and keep iterating until they arrive at a decision.

If this does not describe you (or your company), then you are not data-driven.

&nbsp;
<h2>Summary</h2>
You can't just put up a survey on your website and claim to be data-driven. It takes a bit more than that. And when you do get data, share it with others on your team.

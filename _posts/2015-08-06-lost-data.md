DBAs get paid for performance but we keep our jobs with recovery.

<strong>Being able to recover data is the most important job function you have as a DBA</strong>.  If you can't recover your data because database backups are missing, or corrupt, then you should consider a new line of work.

The year is 2015 and we still have people failing to have a proper recovery plan. I think part of the reason we see such complacency is because data is viewed as an easily replaced commodity, like a server or a hard drive. The reality is that <strong>data is the most important asset your company has</strong>. Data isn't a piece of hardware, it is information. Some people <a href="http://www.computerworld.com/article/2572141/security0/how-much-is-your-data-worth-.html" target="_blank">value their laptop in the millions of dollars</a>, which means the value of your datacenter just skyrocketed.

Imagine for a moment that you came home and discovered that all your data was missing? Music files, movies, video games, banking information, even the deed to your home. All gone. What would you do next? Would you post to a forum asking people to help you recover your lost data?

Well, posting to a forum and saying '<a href="http://stackoverflow.com/questions/14675969/recover-database-with-no-backups" target="_blank">need to recover, no backups available</a>', is about as useful as putting up 'lost data' posters all over town.

Most of us do our best to follow the <a href="http://www.hanselman.com/blog/TheComputerBackupRuleOfThree.aspx" target="_blank">'Computer Backup Rule of Three' principle for our personal data as outlined by Scott Hanselman</a>. If your data is that valuable to yourself, then it is easy to understand how valuable your companies data would be. Treat your company data as if it were your own.

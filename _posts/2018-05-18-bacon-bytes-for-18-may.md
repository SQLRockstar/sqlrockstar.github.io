<h2>Alexa and Siri Can Hear This Hidden Command. You Can’t.</h2>
As if we didn't have enough to worry about when it comes to Alexa commands and security, now we have to worry about <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2018/05/10/technology/alexa-siri-hidden-command-audio-attacks.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">dog-whistle commands being sent to our devices</a>. Fun fact uncovered in this report: <strong>It is not illegal to send subliminal messages to another person, or device</strong>. Broadcast networks discourage the practice, but there is no law on the books. Yet another example of how technology moves too fast for lawmakers. Well, in this case, it's only been 70+ years for television. Makes me wonder what the networks have been doing that we don't know about. Time to <a href="https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0096256/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">watch "They Live" again</a>, I guess.

<h2>EFAIL describes vulnerabilities in the end-to-end encryption technologies OpenPGP and S/MIM</h2>
Nice write up <a href="https://efail.de/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">describing the flaw in email encryption</a> along with possible mitigations for you to try right now. Here's another idea: stop thinking that anything you do online is protected. When you send an email, a copy of that email is likely sitting on every server involved in routing the message. Encrypted or not, that's a lot more exposure than you wanted to know about today.

<h2>You can find whereabouts of any cellphone within seconds</h2>
There are many legitimate cases where you would want law enforcement to be able to track a person to a specific location through their cellphone. The trouble with this service is that it can be abused by law enforcement officials, as is the case <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2018/05/10/technology/cellphone-tracking-law-enforcement.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">in this story</a>. But it's a good reminder that no matter how smart you are with your smartphone privacy, it is likely reporting your location in ways you are not allowed to disable.

<h2>The Entire Economy Is MoviePass Now. Enjoy It While You Can</h2>
<a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2018/05/16/technology/moviepass-economy-startups.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">This article</a> outlines the business model for 98% of every idea that comes out of Silicon Valley. In an effort to build up as many customers they give away their product at a loss. Once they show how many new customers are signing up, they get more funding. They then attract more customers, allowing them to collect more data, which they can then sell in order to recover some of their losses. Then, they alter their product offerings, due to "rising costs", and hope they get more funding, or just bought.
